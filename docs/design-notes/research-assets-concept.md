# Research Assets Concept

## Purpose

Research Assets provide evidence and supporting information for genealogy research.

They help researchers document:

- Where information originates
- Which evidence supports a conclusion
- How research decisions were made

Research Assets are an essential part of genealogy research, but they are not the primary focus of the genealogy graph.

---

# Research Assets Within The Research Model

Ancestra separates genealogy information into three distinct concepts:

Person = Identity

Family = Context

Research Assets = Evidence

Each concept serves a different purpose.

Persons describe who existed.

Families describe how persons are connected.

Research Assets describe why information is believed to be true.

---

# Asset Types

Ancestra currently supports three categories of research assets.

## Sources

Sources describe where genealogy information originates.

Examples:

- Church Records
- Civil Registers
- Census Records
- Military Records
- Family Books

Sources answer:

Where does this information come from?

---

## Media

Media contains files that support genealogy research.

Examples:

- PDF Documents
- Scanned Records
- Photographs
- Maps

Media answer:

What evidence is available?

---

## Notes

Notes contain researcher-authored text.

Examples:

- Research Notes
- Transcriptions
- Interpretations
- Open Questions
- Hypotheses

Notes answer:

What does the researcher know, suspect or want to remember?

---

# Assets Support Research

Research Assets support genealogy research.

They are not the focus of the genealogy graph.

The primary focus remains:

- Persons
- Families
- Relationships

Research Assets provide evidence around those concepts.

---

# Assets Can Belong To Different Contexts

Research Assets may be attached to persons or families.

The attachment location depends on the genealogical meaning of the asset.

Examples:

Person Assets

- Birth Certificate
- Death Certificate
- Portrait Photograph

Family Assets

- Marriage Certificate
- Family Photograph
- Household Census Record

Ancestra does not force researchers into a single organizational model.

Research Assets should be attached where researchers naturally expect them.

---

# Assets Are Contextual

Research Assets are accessed through their research context.

Examples:

Person Card
→ Personal Assets

Family Card
→ Family Assets

Inspector
→ Related Assets

Search
→ Asset Discovery

Assets should remain connected to the context in which they are used.

---

# Assets Are Not Navigation Objects

Researchers navigate through the genealogy graph.

They do not navigate through research assets.

The Navigator primarily exposes:

- Persons
- Families
- Relationships

Research Assets remain accessible through:

- Person Cards
- Family Cards
- Search Results
- Inspector Views

The Navigator should not become a general-purpose asset browser.

---

# Asset Editing

Ancestra follows the principle:

Inline Editing First.

Research Assets should be edited within the research workflow that uses them whenever possible.

Research Asset editing should:

- Minimize context switching
- Stay close to the current research context
- Avoid unnecessary dialogs
- Avoid unnecessary workspace tabs

The research process should remain focused on genealogy rather than asset administration.

---

# Design Goals

Research Assets should:

- Support research
- Document evidence
- Remain easy to attach
- Remain easy to discover
- Remain easy to understand

Research Assets should never distract from the primary goal:

Fast, focused and effective genealogy research.
